Major organs

An organ is two or more tissues working as one unit. Here are the ones you cannot live without — plus the skin and intestines, which people often forget.

Nervous

Brain

About 1.4 kg of tissue containing roughly 86 billion neurons. Controls movement, senses, emotion, memory and every automatic process, from breathing to body temperature.

Uses ~20% of your oxygen and calories

Circulatory

Heart

A four-chambered pump the size of a fist. Beats about 100,000 times a day, pushing 5 litres of blood through two separate circuits.

Resting rate: 60–100 bpm

Respiratory

Lungs

Two spongy organs with around 480 million alveoli. Their total gas-exchange surface is roughly the size of a tennis court.

Right lung has 3 lobes, left has 2

Digestive

Liver

The body's chemical factory: over 500 jobs, including detoxifying blood, storing glycogen, making bile and clotting proteins.

The only organ that can regenerate itself

Urinary

Kidneys

Two bean-shaped filters containing about a million nephrons each. They clean the blood, balance salts and water, and help make red blood cells.

Filter ~180 L of fluid per day

Digestive

Stomach

A muscular bag with a pH of 1.5–3.5. Churns food into chyme and starts protein digestion with pepsin, protected from its own acid by a mucus layer.

Holds roughly 1–1.5 L after a meal

Digestive

Small intestine

Six to seven metres of coiled tube where most nutrient absorption happens. Its inner wall is covered in villi and microvilli.

Surface area of about 30 m2

Integumentary

Skin

The largest organ, around 1.5–2 m2 and several kilograms. A waterproof, self-repairing barrier that senses touch and controls heat loss.

Fully renews itself roughly every 27 days
