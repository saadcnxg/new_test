The human skeleton

206 bones in adults, joined by cartilage, ligaments and tendons. The skeleton supports the body, protects organs, stores minerals and manufactures blood cells.

Two divisions

80 bones

Axial skeleton

The central axis: skull, vertebral column, ribs and sternum. Its job is protection and support.

  • Skull — 22 bones plus 6 ear ossicles
  • Spine — 26 bones (24 vertebrae, sacrum, coccyx)
  • Rib cage — 12 pairs of ribs + sternum
126 bones

Appendicular skeleton

Limbs and the girdles that attach them. Built for movement and grip.

  • Shoulder girdle — clavicle + scapula
  • Arms and hands — 27 bones per hand
  • Pelvis, legs and feet — 26 bones per foot

Five types of bone

Long

Longer than wide, with a shaft and two ends. Femur, humerus, tibia. Levers for movement.

Short

Roughly cube-shaped, for stability and small movements. Wrist and ankle bones.

Flat

Thin and often curved, protecting soft tissue and giving muscle attachment. Skull, ribs, sternum.

Irregular

Complex shapes with several functions. Vertebrae and the mandible.

Sesamoid

Small bones inside tendons that improve leverage. The patella (kneecap) is the largest.

Joints

  • Fibrous — barely movable, like the sutures of the skull.
  • Cartilaginous — slightly movable, like the discs between vertebrae.
  • Synovial — freely movable and lubricated: hinge (knee), ball-and-socket (hip), pivot (neck), saddle (thumb).

Keeping bones strong

  • Weight-bearing exercise builds bone density, especially before age 30.
  • Calcium and vitamin D support mineralisation; vitamin D mainly comes from sunlight.
  • Bone is living tissue: it is constantly broken down and rebuilt, about 10% per year in adults.
