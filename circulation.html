The circulatory system

One pump, two circuits and about 100,000 km of vessels. Everything the body needs is delivered and everything it produces is removed by this single transport network.

The heart

A four-chambered muscle that works in two steps: atria receive, ventricles pump.

Right atrium

Receives deoxygenated blood

Blood returns from the body through the vena cava and is pushed into the right ventricle.

Right ventricle

Pumps to the lungs

Thinner wall, because the pulmonary circuit is short and low-pressure.

Left atrium

Receives oxygenated blood

Oxygen-rich blood arrives from the lungs through the pulmonary veins.

Left ventricle

Pumps to the body

The thickest chamber — it drives blood through the entire systemic circuit.

Valves between the chambers close when pressure flips, producing the "lub-dub" sound. A resting heart moves about 5 litres per minute; during hard exercise this can rise above 20.

Two circuits

Short loop

Pulmonary circuit

Right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium. Its only job is gas exchange.

Long loop

Systemic circuit

Left ventricle → aorta → body tissues → vena cava → right atrium. Delivers oxygen and fuel, collects waste.

What is in blood?

  • Plasma — about 55% of blood volume; water, salts, proteins and dissolved nutrients.
  • Red blood cells — carry oxygen using haemoglobin; live around 120 days.
  • White blood cells — the immune defenders, from neutrophils to lymphocytes.
  • Platelets — cell fragments that clump together to seal a damaged vessel.

Supporting your circulation

  • Move regularly — skeletal muscle squeezes veins and helps blood return.
  • Keep sodium moderate; high salt raises blood pressure over time.
  • Avoid smoking, which stiffens vessels and reduces oxygen delivery.
