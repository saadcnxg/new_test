The eleven systems of the human body

A system is a group of organs that cooperate on one job. Most systems overlap: the heart needs the lungs, the muscles need the nerves, and the kidneys answer to hormones from the endocrine glands.

Skeletal

Framework & protection

206 bones, cartilage and ligaments. Supports the body, protects organs, stores calcium and produces blood cells in the marrow.

Key parts: skull, spine, ribs, pelvis, limbs

Muscular

Movement & heat

Over 600 skeletal muscles pull on bones to create movement. Smooth muscle moves food and blood; cardiac muscle pumps the heart.

Key parts: biceps, diaphragm, heart wall, gut wall

Nervous

Control & signalling

Brain, spinal cord and nerves send electrical messages at up to 120 m/s. It handles thought, memory, senses and automatic reflexes.

Key parts: brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves

Circulatory

Transport

Heart, blood and vessels deliver oxygen and nutrients and carry away carbon dioxide and waste. See the dedicated page.

Key parts: heart, arteries, veins, capillaries

Respiratory

Gas exchange

Air travels down the trachea into 480 million alveoli, where oxygen enters the blood and CO2 leaves. Roughly 12–20 breaths per minute at rest.

Key parts: nose, trachea, bronchi, lungs

Digestive

Fuel & absorption

A 9-metre tube plus organs that add enzymes. Food is broken down, nutrients absorbed in the small intestine, water reclaimed in the colon.

Key parts: mouth, stomach, small intestine, liver

Urinary

Filtration

Kidneys filter about 180 litres of fluid a day and return most of it. Waste leaves as urine, while water and salt balance is fine-tuned.

Key parts: kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra

Endocrine

Hormones

Glands release hormones into the blood to control growth, metabolism, stress and reproduction — slower than nerves, but longer lasting.

Key parts: pituitary, thyroid, adrenals, pancreas

Lymphatic & immune

Defence & drainage

Lymph vessels return leaked fluid to the blood. White blood cells identify and destroy pathogens, and lymph nodes act as checkpoints.

Key parts: lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, tonsils

Integumentary

Skin, hair, nails

The body's largest organ system. Blocks pathogens and UV light, holds in water, and regulates temperature through sweat and blood flow.

Key parts: epidermis, dermis, sweat glands

Reproductive

New life

Produces gametes and sex hormones. The only system not required for the survival of an individual, but essential for the species.

Key parts: ovaries, testes, uterus

Systems do not work alone

Two examples of crosstalk:

  • Exercise: muscles demand oxygen → breathing rate rises (respiratory) → heart rate rises (circulatory) → heat is lost through sweat (integumentary).
  • Stress: the brain signals the adrenal glands (endocrine) → adrenaline raises heart rate (circulatory) → blood is redirected to muscles (muscular).

Homeostasis is the name for all of this balancing: the body holding temperature, pH, blood sugar and water near a set point.
